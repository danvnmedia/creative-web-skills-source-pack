# Motion Performance Auditor — Benchmark & Showcase Sources

A beautiful showcase is also an audit target: learn what survives real constraints, not just what looks impressive in a desktop recording.

| Source | Audit lens |
|---|---|
| Framer Awards — https://www.framer.com/awards/ | Does strong motion remain readable and usable? |
| Codrops Creative Hub — https://tympanus.net/codrops/hub/ | Compare a focused demo's cost with what is appropriate in a full production page |
| R3F examples — https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/getting-started/examples | Validate idiomatic scene/render-loop patterns |
| Three.js examples — https://threejs.org/examples/ | Compare technique complexity and rendering alternatives |

| MDN prefers-reduced-motion — https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion | Ground reduced-motion behavior |

## Showcase audit exercise

When reviewing an impressive site, do not stop at “smooth on my laptop.” Check:

- first meaningful/LCP content before spectacle
- input responsiveness while animation runs
- behavior during reverse scrolling
- memory/render-loop behavior after navigation
- mobile thermal/frame-time pressure
- touch and keyboard alternatives
- reduced-motion design
- static/poster fallback when canvas fails

The goal is not to make every creative site cheap; it is to spend the performance budget where the experience actually benefits.
